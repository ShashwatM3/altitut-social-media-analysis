# CONTENT PACK 01 — Startup Teardown

## 1. OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Series Name + Premise
**"Startup Teardown"** — A social media team member breaks down a real, recognizable startup on a whiteboard, dissecting its origin story and strategy using Altitude's own startup-building framework as the lens.

**One-line hook concept:** *"We reverse-engineer how [famous startup] actually made it — using the same framework you can use to build yours."*

### 1.2 Format & Platform
- **Format:** UGC talking-head + whiteboard breakdown, short-form vertical video
- **Length:** 45–90 seconds
- **Platforms:** Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts (cross-posted, platform-native captions per channel)
- **Visual mode:** Live whiteboard sketching in-frame (not pre-drawn), team member speaking directly to camera throughout

### 1.3 Origin
This series comes from the whitespace observation that competitors talk *about* startup success in the abstract (motivational quotes, generic tips) but rarely do a structured, franchise-able teardown of specific companies using a repeatable framework. It also directly showcases Altitude's own product framework in action — every teardown is a live demo of the tool, disguised as entertaining, educational content. Cross-reference: pull specific competitor content gaps from the Competitor Packs panel to prioritize which startups/angles to cover first (e.g., if a competitor is covering "funding stories" but ignoring "GTM strategy," lean into GTM teardowns).

---

## 2. STRATEGY

### 2.1 What It Promotes
The Altitude framework itself — the structured process the platform uses to guide a user from idea to startup. Each episode implicitly sells the framework by using it as the analytical lens, without ever feeling like an ad.

### 2.2 Goal
Primary: **Awareness + Engagement** (comments, saves, shares driven by recognizable startup names and "insider breakdown" appeal)
Secondary: **Conversion** via CTA into template/lead capture (comment-to-DM funnel)

### 2.3 Who It's For
Aspiring founders, early-stage builders, and startup-curious professionals who follow business/startup content but haven't yet started building — people who consume "startup lore" content (a la trend of business breakdown accounts) and are primed to want a practical next step.

## 3. THE SERIES → WHAT TO MAKE

### Episode 1
- **3.1.1 Title / Angle:** "How [Startup A] Found Its First 1,000 Users"
- **3.1.2 Hook:** "This company had zero users and no marketing budget. Here's exactly what they did." (whiteboard: draw a big "0" crossed out)
- **3.1.3 What It Shows:** Sketch founder + problem → sketch target audience box → walk through their specific early distribution tactic (e.g., community seeding, cold outreach, a clever hack) → circle the one repeatable principle
- **3.1.4 CTA:** "Comment 'BUILD' and I'll send you the exact framework I just used to break this down."

### Episode 2
- **3.2.1 Title / Angle:** "The Positioning Trick [Startup B] Used to Beat Bigger Competitors"
- **3.2.2 Hook:** "They were the smallest player in the room. Here's how they won anyway."
- **3.2.3 What It Shows:** Sketch the competitive landscape (big players vs. the underdog) → break down their specific positioning/story choice → show how it mapped to a clear ICP → land on the takeaway
- **3.2.4 CTA:** "Comment 'TEARDOWN' for the fill-in-the-blank version of this breakdown."

### Episode 3
- **3.3.1 Title / Angle:** "The Go-To-Market Move That Made [Startup C] Blow Up"
- **3.3.2 Hook:** "One channel. That's all they used to get their first 10,000 customers."
- **3.3.3 What It Shows:** Sketch the funnel → isolate the single GTM channel/tactic → explain why it worked for their specific audience → note what *wouldn't* have worked and why
- **3.3.4 CTA:** "Comment 'FRAMEWORK' and I'll send the worksheet I just used."

*(Additional episodes follow the same skeleton — new startup, new framework category each time: valuation strategy, pricing model, founding team dynamics, pivot stories, fundraising narrative, etc. Maintain a rolling backlog of 10–15 pre-approved startups so episodes can be batched.)*

## 4. THE RECIPE → HOW TO MAKE

### 4.1 Structure
- **Hook (0–3s):** Bold claim or surprising stat about the startup, delivered to camera before any whiteboard action
- **Setup (3–15s):** Who/what/problem — sketched in real time
- **Body / Breakdown (15–60s):** 2–4 framework categories walked through with sketches (target audience, strategy, key bet, growth loop — pick 2–3 per episode, don't try to cover everything)
- **Takeaway (60–75s):** One circled, underlined principle — the "steal this" moment
- **CTA (75–90s):** Direct ask, tied to a specific comment keyword
- **Target length:** 60–90 seconds

### 4.2 Visual Style
- Physical whiteboard (or tablet/stylus for a cleaner digital-whiteboard look — pick one and stay consistent across the series)
- Team member always in-frame, talking while drawing (not voiceover over static drawing)
- On-screen text: episode number badge (e.g., "Teardown #14") in a consistent corner position for series recognition
- Consistent color scheme for markers/highlights (e.g., always circle the "takeaway" in the same color)

### 4.3 Audio
- Direct-to-camera voice as primary audio (not lip-synced trend audio — authenticity matters for this format)
- Light, low-key background music bed (business/focus genre, low volume) to avoid dead air without competing with speech
- Avoid trending meme sounds — this series' credibility depends on feeling like genuine expertise, not entertainment mimicry

### 4.4 Caption + Hashtags
**Caption template:**
> Teardown #[X]: How [Startup] did [specific thing]. 🧵 Full breakdown ⬆️
> Want the framework? Comment '[KEYWORD]' 👇

**Hashtag set (mix of broad + niche, 5–8 tags):**
`#StartupTeardown #FounderStory #StartupStrategy #BuildInPublic #[StartupName] #Entrepreneurship #GTMStrategy #StartupTips`

## 5. EXECUTION

### 5.1 Cadence
1–2x per week (this series requires more research/prep per episode than Pack #2, so lower frequency is appropriate)

### 5.2 Roles & Effort
- **On-camera talent:** 1 team member (consistent face for series recognition)
- **Research/scripting:** Light-to-moderate — requires verifying public facts about each startup before recording (avoid unverified claims or anything that could read as disparaging)
- **Filming:** Single take or few takes, minimal editing (raw whiteboard format is part of the appeal)
- **Difficulty:** Medium (mainly due to research burden, not production complexity)

### 5.3 What Good Looks Like
Watch **comment-to-view ratio** and **keyword-comment volume** (i.e., how many people actually type the CTA keyword) as the core signal — this tells you the content is landing *and* the CTA is converting attention into a lead, not just passive engagement. Saves are a secondary signal worth tracking (indicates "reference value" and feeds the algorithm's distribution).
