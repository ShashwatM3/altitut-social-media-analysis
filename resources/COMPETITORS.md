# COMPETITORS.md - Altitut Platform Competitors

*Anchored on what Altitut actually is: a digital platform where students and early founders learn AND build a startup through structured, sequentially-unlocking modules and interactive tools (Ikigai idea discovery, customer-interview workflow, persona builder, MVP builder, pitch review/craft/practice, funding discovery, badges/credentials), plus a gamified 2D RPG version with XP, coins, pets, and mini-games. A real competitor is therefore another software platform where users learn entrepreneurship by doing it inside the product, ideally gamified and aimed at students or first-time founders. Not a person, not a podcast, not a course-content or media brand. Follower counts are snapshots found via web search on 2026-07-09.*

## The honest headline

Two things are true at once, and they pull against each other:

1. There ARE genuine platform competitors: gamified and structured entrepreneurship-learning software where users build a startup inside the product. They are listed below.
2. Almost all of them have small social followings. They are niche edtech companies, so their Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube presences are tiny (often under a few thousand, sometimes a single Facebook page). The one partial exception is Fe/male Switch, which runs a real content and SEO engine.

The reason big social accounts kept surfacing in earlier passes is that the only entities in this space with millions of followers are creators and media brands (Hormozi, Noah Kagan, Starter Story, YC). Those are not platforms and are not competitors, so they are excluded here.

Practical implication for the Social Media Command Center: study the competitors below for product, positioning, and gamification, but do not expect to copy their social playbooks, because most barely have one. For social and content mechanics specifically, the realistic models are adjacent (Fe/male Switch's content operation, and, purely as craft references, big gamified-education and entrepreneurship-education accounts). Those are in a clearly separated section at the end so they are never mistaken for competitors.

## Per-competitor detail (top matches)

### 1. Fe/male Switch (F/MS) - closest match
- What it is: A women-first startup RPG and simulator. Core surfaces read almost like Altitut's: a Startup Simulator (role-play as founder), a Skill Lab (learn via micro-modules, complete quests, track progress), a Sandbox that walks you through structured blocks (identify problem, define idea, understand audience, product development, validation) with an AI co-founder (Elona), a Marketplace with in-game tokens, a community, AI-generated personalized roadmaps, tiered access (START, LEARN, PLAY), and self-updating "live certificate" micro-credentials.
- Websites: https://femaleswitch.com, https://femaleswitch.app
- Social: LinkedIn https://www.linkedin.com/company/femaleswitch, plus X and Instagram (they name these as their primary community channels). The founder ("Mean CEO," Violetta Bonenkamp) runs a heavy SEO and content operation (blog.mean.ceo and the femaleswitch.com blog), which is why F/MS is the one true competitor with a real content presence to study.
- Why it matters most: It is the closest thing to "Altitut for aspiring female founders." Study its game-to-module structure, its Sandbox validation flow, its live-credential system, and especially its content and SEO engine, which is the most transferable social lesson in this whole set.

### 2. Startup Wars
- What it is: A browser-based, gamified startup simulation platform. Students launch virtual businesses across industries, build MVPs, identify customer segments, manage cash flow and budgets, and pivot, with real-time feedback and instructor analytics. Includes an AI entrepreneurship coach (Milton). Sold into high school and district CTE, business, and entrepreneurship courses.
- Websites: https://www.startupwars.com, portal at https://portal.startupwars.com
- Social: Facebook https://www.facebook.com/startupwarsapp/ (~124 likes). Very small.
- Why it matters: The closest match to Altitut's game-plus-course-embedded model with a student audience and an AI coach. Strong product benchmark, weak social benchmark.

### 3. The U App
- What it is: Positions itself directly as "the gamified entrepreneurial learning platform," offering gamified courses, cohort programs, live sessions, and a community.
- Websites: https://theu.app
- Social: Thin public footprint; could not verify meaningful accounts.
- Why it matters: The on-the-nose category match by name and positioning. Worth a direct look at the product; low value as a social model.

### 6. IdeaBuddy
- What it is: AI-powered, all-in-one startup-build software. A step-by-step Guide (modules), Idea Plan Canvas, Financial Plan, idea Validation, an auto-assembled Business Plan, a Whiteboard, and pitch support. Explicitly designed for entrepreneurs and for business schools that fold it into an entrepreneurship curriculum.
- Websites: https://ideabuddy.com
- Social: Instagram https://www.instagram.com/ideabuddy/ (~1K), plus LinkedIn, YouTube, and Facebook. Small but genuinely multi-platform and consistently active, so it is a reasonable small-account model.
- Why it matters: Closest analog to Altitut's "do the work" web app (guided modules plus tools), minus the game.

### 7. Disciplined Entrepreneurship (MIT Orbit / DE Toolbox)
- What it is: The digital home of MIT's 24-step Disciplined Entrepreneurship methodology. Orbit is the platform; DE Toolbox provides the tools, dashboards, and course-integration (assign the methodology, track each student's progress, assign mentors); JetPack adds AI assistants across the steps. Used at MIT and licensed to universities.
- Websites: https://www.detoolbox.com, https://orbit.mit.edu, https://www.d-eship.com
- Social: Modest. There is no large brand social account; the reach lives on Bill Aulet's LinkedIn and the d-eship.com blog.
- Why it matters: Arguably the closest to Altitut's structured-curriculum-plus-tools-plus-course-embedding web app. A top product benchmark, not a social one.

## Social and content models (NOT competitors - use only to study content craft)

Because the real competitors are weak on social, and your goal is to learn social and content strategy, here are the two best craft references. They are explicitly NOT competitors to Altitut and should never be listed as such; they are only worth studying for how they make content.

- Foundr (https://foundr.com, IG @foundr ~3.6M): a media and course company for entrepreneurs. Not a build-a-startup platform, but the strongest entrepreneurship-education content operation to study for hooks, carousels, and reels.
- Duolingo (https://www.duolingo.com, TikTok @duolingo ~16M+): a gamified learning app. Not a competitor in subject, but the single best model in the world for marketing a gamified learning product, which is directly relevant to Altitut's RPG, XP, pet, and leaderboard mechanics.

## Considered and Excluded

- Alex Hormozi, Noah Kagan, GaryVee, Starter Story, Pat Walls - people and media or content brands, not platforms where users build a startup. Excluded as competitors (Starter Story and Foundr can be studied only as content craft).
- Y Combinator, Techstars, Antler, 500, Founder Institute - accelerators and funds; their value is capital, not a learning platform. (Startup School is a course, not a build-it platform.)
- Coursera, Udemy, edX, Skillshare, Maven - horizontal course platforms, not startup-building tools.
- Offline youth programs (NFTE, INCubatoredu, Leangap, VentureLab, Junior Achievement, Young Founders School) - entrepreneurship education, but delivered as in-person programs, not as a platform product.
- Notion, Figma, Airtable - productivity tools, wrong motive.
- Altitut itself and its properties - self-match.

## Notes for Prompt 2 (social scraping)

- The competitors worth scraping for social are limited by reality. Fe/male Switch (content and SEO, LinkedIn, X, Instagram) is the only true competitor with a content engine worth analyzing. IdeaBuddy (IG, LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook) is a usable small-account model. Strategyzer is LinkedIn-only.
- Startup Wars, The U App, SimVenture, GoVenture, Disciplined Entrepreneurship, and Virtonomics have little or no scrapeable consumer social; treat them as product benchmarks, not social ones.
- If the pipeline needs large accounts to learn content mechanics, scrape the two content models (Foundr IG, Duolingo TikTok and IG), but keep them tagged as content-model, not competitor.
- Handles marked as likely (Fe/male Switch IG and X, IdeaBuddy IG) should be confirmed before scraping.
