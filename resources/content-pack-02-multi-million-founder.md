# CONTENT PACK 02 — Becoming a Multi-Million Dollar Founder, One Day at a Time

## 1. OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Series Name + Premise
**"Becoming a Multi-Million Dollar Founder, One Day at a Time"** — A high-frequency, glossary-style series where a team member explains one startup-world *term* per episode (e.g., valuation, GTM strategy, cap table, runway) and grounds it in a real example of a company that used it well.

**One-line hook concept:** *"One startup term a day, explained in under a minute — with proof it actually works."*

### 1.2 Format & Platform
- **Format:** UGC talking-head + whiteboard or slide/graphic visuals, short-form vertical video
- **Length:** 30–60 seconds (shorter than Pack #1 — designed for high frequency and quick consumption)
- **Platforms:** Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts
- **Visual mode:** Flexible per episode — whiteboard sketch OR clean text/graphic slide, but should stay consistent within the series (pick one primary mode, use the other sparingly for variety)

### 1.3 Origin
This series fills a whitespace where competitors either avoid startup education entirely or bury it in long-form content (blog posts, YouTube videos 10+ minutes long) that doesn't fit short-form, habitual scrolling behavior. It also builds a compounding content asset: over time, this series becomes a searchable, saveable "startup glossary" that's a lead-gen funnel into Altitude's framework — directly complementary to the Startup Teardown pack (Pack #1), which goes deep on *specific companies* while this one goes deep on *specific terms*.

---

## 2. STRATEGY

### 2.1 What It Promotes
Founder literacy and, by extension, Altitude's positioning as the platform that helps people not just build a startup but *understand the language of building one*. Indirectly funnels into the same framework/template offer as Pack #1, reinforcing cross-series brand consistency.

### 2.2 Goal
Primary: **Awareness + habitual engagement** (daily/near-daily cadence builds a following that returns for each "day," similar to "day in the life" or countdown series psychology)
Secondary: **Conversion** via CTA, and long-term **evergreen search/save value** since glossary-style content stays relevant indefinitely

### 2.3 Who It's For
Early-stage and aspiring founders who feel behind on "startup vocabulary" — people newer to the startup world who want quick, credible, bite-sized education rather than long-form content, and who are likely to binge a numbered series once they discover it.

---

## 3. THE SERIES → WHAT TO MAKE

### Episode — Day 1
- **3.1.1 Title / Angle:** "Day 1: Valuation — What It Actually Means"
- **3.1.2 Hook:** "If you don't understand this term, you'll get a bad deal in your first raise."
- **3.1.3 What It Shows:** Plain-English definition of valuation → why it matters at the earliest stage → real example of a startup whose valuation strategy/story is well documented → one-line practical takeaway
- **3.1.4 CTA:** "Comment 'DAY 1' and I'll send you today's term as a one-pager."

### Episode — Day 2
- **3.2.1 Title / Angle:** "Day 2: Go-To-Market Strategy — The Term Everyone Uses Wrong"
- **3.2.2 Hook:** "Most founders think GTM means 'marketing.' It doesn't. Here's the real definition."
- **3.2.3 What It Shows:** Clarify the common misconception → correct definition → real-world example of a company's specific GTM choice and why it worked for their audience → takeaway
- **3.2.4 CTA:** "Comment 'DAY 2' for the one-pager."

### Episode — Day 3
- **3.3.1 Title / Angle:** "Day 3: Runway — The Number That Decides If You Survive"
- **3.3.2 Hook:** "This is the first number every investor will ask you about."
- **3.3.3 What It Shows:** Definition (cash on hand ÷ burn rate) → why it's the single most important early-stage metric → example of a startup that extended runway smartly (or a cautionary example of running out) → takeaway
- **3.3.4 CTA:** "Comment 'DAY 3' for the one-pager."

*(Subsequent days rotate through a pre-built term bank: CAC, LTV, cap table, MVP, product-market fit, burn rate, TAM/SAM/SOM, equity dilution, term sheet, churn, etc. Maintain a running numbered backlog of 30–60 terms so the team is never scrambling day-of, and so the series can visibly "count up" for binge/completionist appeal.)*

## 4. THE RECIPE → HOW TO MAKE

### 4.1 Structure
- **Hook (0–3s):** State the term like a secret or a warning — creates urgency/curiosity immediately
- **Body (3–35s):** Plain-English definition → why it matters for a founder specifically → one real-company example
- **Takeaway (35–45s):** One-line, highly quotable distillation of the term
- **CTA (45–60s):** Comment-the-day-number ask
- **Target length:** 30–60 seconds (shorter than Pack #1 by design — this series lives on frequency, not depth)

### 4.2 Visual Style
- Consistent visual mode per series (whiteboard OR slide graphics — do not mix week-to-week)
- **"Day [X]" badge** always visible on-screen — this is the single most important visual element, as it's what makes the series feel like a countable, collectible franchise
- Consistent typography/color for the term itself when it appears on-screen (bold, large, one-word-or-phrase reveal)

### 4.3 Audio
- Fast-paced direct-to-camera delivery — should feel energetic and quick given the short runtime
- Upbeat, minimal background music bed; can lean slightly more trend-aware than Pack #1 since this series is more "snackable" and less "deep dive," but should not rely on meme sounds that date the content (glossary content should stay evergreen)

### 4.4 Caption + Hashtags
**Caption template:**
> Day [X]: [Term]. Here's what it actually means (and who's used it right). 📈
> Want the one-pager? Comment 'DAY [X]' 👇
> Building the full glossary — comment 'GLOSSARY' to get the whole list as we go.

**Hashtag set (mix of broad + niche, 5–8 tags):**
`#StartupTerms #FounderLife #StartupGlossary #BuildInPublic #Entrepreneurship #StartupTips #ValuationExplained #DayInTheLife`
*(swap the term-specific tag each episode, e.g. `#ValuationExplained` → `#RunwayExplained`)*

## 5. EXECUTION

### 5.1 Cadence
Daily or every 2nd–3rd day — this is the defining feature of the series and should not be allowed to slip, since the "Day X" numbering only works if the cadence is reliable and visibly consistent to followers.

### 5.2 Roles & Effort
- **On-camera talent:** 1 team member (can rotate, but consistency helps series recognition — same guidance as Pack #1)
- **Research/scripting:** Low per episode (definitions are stable/factual; only the example needs light verification), but requires upfront investment in building the full term bank and backlog before launch
- **Filming:** Very low production overhead by design — should be batchable (e.g., film 5–10 episodes in a single session) to sustain the daily cadence without daily filming
- **Difficulty:** Low-to-medium (the challenge is operational consistency and backlog management, not creative or research difficulty)

### 5.3 What Good Looks Like
Track **save rate** as the primary signal (glossary content is meant to be referenced later — high saves indicate the format is delivering real reference value) alongside **CTA keyword-comment volume per episode** to see which specific terms generate the most lead interest (useful for prioritizing which terms to expand into deeper content, e.g., feeding a high-performing term back into a Pack #1 Startup Teardown episode).
